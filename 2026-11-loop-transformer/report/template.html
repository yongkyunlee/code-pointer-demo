Mechanistic interpretability

A carry through a looped Transformer

Two small Transformers learned the same addition problems. Swapping an early hidden state shifted the answer further in the model that reused its weights. By the final stage, the average difference had reversed.

3 training seeds4 stages128 held-out sum pairs

Controlled architecture comparison

Two models, the same sums

Both models executed eight blocks in four stages. One reused the same two blocks at every stage. The other had eight independent blocks. Within each training seed they started with identical outputs and saw the same 763,200 tokens. Their clean tens-digit accuracies were 96.3% and 95.8%, respectively. A third, narrower untied model with a parameter count close to the tied model’s reached 97.9%.

For each test pair, the sums had the same total, but only one needed a carry from the ones column. The experiment replaced a hidden state in one sum with the corresponding state from the other. If the carry transfers and the tens operands stay put, the predicted tens digit shifts by one.

At stage one, that target digit gained 0.400 logits relative to the original digit in the tied model and 0.080 in the untied model. All three seeds favored the tied model here. At stage four, two of the three favored the untied model.

Pretrained models · arithmetic

Reading a carry in two languages

Ouro-1.4B runs 24 blocks four times. Qwen2.5-1.5B runs 28 distinct blocks, divided here into four stages. They differ in training, tokenizer and executed depth, so a difference between them cannot isolate weight sharing.

A classifier trained on stage-three hidden states could tell whether a sum needed a carry in 85–93% of held-out prompts. The models’ own first words were more uneven. Ouro gave the correct tens word in English on 98 of 100 sums and in French on 10.

Pretrained models · perspective

Three views of a moving coin

A coin moves between three boxes. Alice and Bob witness different moves. Across 120 scenes, each model answered three questions per scene about the two people’s beliefs and the coin’s actual location.

Once the story was hidden, neither model ranked all three answers correctly in any scene. Swapping in stored states from a different ending changed the top-ranked location in 0 of 120 scenes for each model. The pilot recall checks ruled out the planned two-room follow-up.

Pretrained models · retrieval

A second copy of the clue

Each locker record named two fruits. Ouro ranked the right fruit higher in 93 of 100 scenes with the record repeated, yet it produced that fruit as its next token in only 45. Qwen’s two scores were 55 and zero.

Blocking access to the clue at every layer hurt both models. Blocking only the two selected visits to a reused Ouro block gave nearly the same interaction for the relevant and irrelevant clues. Their difference was 0.00008 logits, with a 95% interval from −0.00313 to +0.00344.
