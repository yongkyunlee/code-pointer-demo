Controlled architecture comparison

Same depth, different weights

A Transformer block contains attention and a feed-forward network. The looped model applied the same pair of blocks four times. The regular model applied four independently trainable pairs once each. Both models started with identical outputs and saw the same training tokens within each seed. Both learned the tens digit, reaching 96.3% clean accuracy with tied weights and 95.8% with independent weights. A narrower untied model with a similar parameter count to the tied model reached 97.9%.

A swap after the first stage pushed the final score further toward the donor’s carry in the tied model. The difference was +0.320 logits, and all three training seeds agreed on its direction. By the fourth stage the average had reversed, and the seeds disagreed.

The same two architectures on all three tasks

These small models learned the three tasks using a shared vocabulary of symbolic tokens. Ouro and Qwen answered natural-language prompts in the checkpoint experiments.

Scoring and model sizes

Each carry family contributed both transfer directions in English and French. The shift measures the final score of the carry-predicted digit relative to the original digit. Each coin family supplied three questions under each of two witness assignments. After block 4, all three questions received the same eight stored states. We scored the first-choice token after block 8. Each two-person locker family supplied eight versions, varying the two people’s locker assignments and clue order. Success required the correct fruit to be the model’s first-choice token.

Both models in the table use width 64 and execute eight blocks. Reusing a pair of blocks reduces the number of trainable parameters. A separate regular model with width 32 brought its parameter count closer to the looped model’s and used less computation per block.

Their pretraining, tokenizers and executed depths also differ. These runs show how the two checkpoints behaved. The small models above give the comparison at matched width, depth and training.

Compare the intact and blocked runs at every depth ↑

Across all 100 locker records

Ouro ranked the right fruit higher in 93 scenes, yet it produced that fruit as its next token in only 45. Qwen’s two scores were 55 and zero.

Cutting that same attention link at every layer reduced the correct fruit’s lead in both models. To test whether a second visit recovered the clue, we measured how much more the later cut cost when we also made the early cut. The Ouro-minus-Qwen difference was +0.00273 logits (95% paired interval −0.00008 to +0.00578). These data leave a recovery advantage for the looped checkpoint unresolved.
