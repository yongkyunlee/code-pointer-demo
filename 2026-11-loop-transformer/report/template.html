Controlled architecture comparison

Same depth, different weights

A Transformer block contains attention and a feed-forward network. The looped model applied the same pair of blocks four times. The regular model applied four independently trainable pairs once each. Both models started with identical outputs and saw the same training tokens within each seed. Both learned the tens digit, reaching 96.3% clean accuracy with tied weights and 95.8% with independent weights. A narrower untied model with a similar parameter count to the tied model reached 97.9%.

A swap after the first stage pushed the final score further toward the donor’s carry in the tied model. The difference was +0.320 logits, and all three training seeds agreed on its direction. By the fourth stage the average had reversed, and the seeds disagreed.

Pretrained models · arithmetic

Reading a carry in two languages

For each model, the classifier reads the state at the swap location shown above. Each row compares what the classifier could decode at that point with the tens word the model produced on the clean prompt.

A classifier trained on stage-three hidden states could tell whether a sum needed a carry in 85–93% of held-out prompts. The models’ own first words were more uneven. Ouro gave the correct tens word in English on 98 of 100 sums and in French on 10.

Pretrained models · perspective

Three views of a moving coin

A coin moves between three boxes. Alice and Bob witness different moves. Across 120 scenes, each model answered three questions per scene about the two people’s beliefs and the coin’s actual location.

{{MEMORY_PATH}} {{MEMORY_SCENE}} Trace the actual-location question through every block ↑

With eight stored positions, both models finished near three-choice chance. The 95% scene-bootstrap intervals were 30.0–34.2% for Ouro and 30.6–34.4% for Qwen. Neither model ranked all three answers correctly in a scene. Substituting states from a different ending changed the top-ranked box for the actual-location question in 0 of 120 scenes in either model.

Pretrained models · retrieval

A second copy of the clue

Each locker record placed a fruit in each of two lockers and assigned each person a locker. The model saw the record twice. We blocked the part of the second copy that describes the lockers from reading a person’s assignment in the first copy at selected depths.

{{LOCKER_SCENE}} Compare the intact and blocked runs at every depth ↑

Across all 100 locker records

Ouro ranked the right fruit higher in 93 scenes, yet it produced that fruit as its next token in only 45. Qwen’s two scores were 55 and zero.

Cutting that same attention link at every layer reduced the correct fruit’s lead in both models. To test whether a second visit recovered the clue, we measured how much more the later cut cost when we also made the early cut. The Ouro-minus-Qwen difference was +0.00273 logits (95% paired interval −0.00008 to +0.00578). These data leave a recovery advantage for the looped checkpoint unresolved.
